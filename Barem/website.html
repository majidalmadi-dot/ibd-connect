Built around your child's gut health

Not a generic tracker — every screen is shaped for parents: symptoms, feeding, growth, development and knowing when to seek care.

👶

Multi-child diary

Add each child once. Age-aware loggers switch automatically — infant feeds & diaper stools for babies, Bristol & pain faces for older kids.

📈

WHO growth charts

Plot weight, height, head circumference and BMI on official WHO percentile curves — the standards Saudi MOH adopted for under-5s — and see your child's percentile.

⭐

Milestones

Age-banded developmental checklists (2 months to 5 years) across social, language and movement — framed as “most children by this age,” never a test.

📚

Condition library

Plain-language guides for constipation, reflux, colic, tummy pain, IBD, coeliac, EoE, food allergy and more — each with signs, triggers and red flags.

🥣

Feeding & safety

Weaning by age, Gulf-food examples, Ramadan guidance for older children, quick calculators (fluids, ORS, fibre) — and an always-visible “when to seek urgent care.”

📤

Share with your doctor

Generate a clean summary of symptoms, stool patterns, growth and medicines to make every clinic visit count — a summary to share, not a diagnosis.

🚻

Find a bathroom fast

Out with a child who has urgency, constipation or IBD? One tap finds the nearest restroom, mosque or mall on the map — because you can’t always plan it.

🎨

Make it yours

Eight colour themes, light & dark mode, a custom accent colour, and adjustable text size — plus the app updates itself automatically when there’s something new.

Baraʿem · براعم

Baraʿem is a non-medical health companion for parents, for personal tracking and education about children's digestive health. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment and is not a substitute for professional care. Growth charts and milestones are educational and are interpreted by your care team. If your child has green (bilious) vomiting, blood in vomit or stool, trouble breathing, a stuck swallowed object, severe pain, or signs of dehydration, seek urgent care. Always consult your child's healthcare provider.
