EoE is rising — and often found late

Eosinophilic esophagitis is a chronic, allergy-driven inflammation of the food pipe. People quietly adapt — chewing longer, drinking with every bite, avoiding meat or bread — so it goes unnoticed for years, while untreated inflammation can slowly narrow the esophagus.

Understand it
EoE, explained simply

In EoE, a type of white blood cell — the eosinophil — builds up in the lining of the esophagus, usually in response to foods or airborne allergens. The result is swelling, rings and narrowing that make food feel stuck.

🔎Symptoms

What it can feel like

  • Food — especially meat, rice or bread — sticking on the way down.
  • Chest discomfort or heartburn that does not settle with usual reflux treatment.
  • Hidden adaptations: eating slowly, cutting food small, drinking after every bite.
  • In children: feeding difficulty, vomiting, abdominal pain or poor growth.
Food stuck and you can't swallow your saliva? That is an emergency — go to the nearest emergency department.
🔬Diagnosis

How it is confirmed

EoE is diagnosed with an upper endoscopy and biopsies. Doctors look for typical features (rings, furrows, white spots, swelling, narrowing — summarised as the EREFS score) and count eosinophils under the microscope: 15 or more per high-power field, together with symptoms, supports the diagnosis once other causes are excluded. Follow-up endoscopies check whether treatment is working.

Treatment rests on the 3 D’s
💊

Drugs

Acid-suppressing PPIs, swallowed topical steroids, and — for some — a biologic such as dupilumab. Chosen and dosed only by your doctor.

🥗

Diet

Removing the most common trigger foods, then reintroducing them one by one with endoscopy to find your personal triggers — ideally with a dietitian.

🎈

Dilation

When the esophagus has narrowed, it can be gently stretched during endoscopy. It relieves swallowing but does not treat the inflammation, so drugs or diet continue.

Questions
Frequently asked
Is Maeen a medical device?
No. Maeen is a non-medical companion for personal tracking and education. It does not diagnose, prescribe diets or drug doses, or replace your gastroenterologist, allergist or dietitian. Everything it shows is descriptive.
Is EoE the same as reflux (GERD)?
No, although they can overlap and both may cause heartburn. EoE is an allergic-type inflammation confirmed by biopsy; reflux is caused by stomach acid flowing back. Only your doctor can tell them apart.
Which treatment is right for me?
Drugs, diet and dilation can be used alone or together, and the choice depends on your symptoms, endoscopy, lifestyle and preferences. Maeen helps you learn about each and bring good questions to your visit.
What happens to my data?
Everything you log stays on your device by default — no account required. Optional cloud backup is end-to-end encrypted with a code only you hold. You can export or delete everything at any time, and you choose when to share a doctor summary.
Is it free, and does it work offline?
Yes to both. Maeen is completely free and installable — open it in your browser or add it to your home screen, and it keeps working offline. It is fully bilingual with right-to-left support.
